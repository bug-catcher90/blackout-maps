Water When the Tap Stops is the water guide: how much to store, how to make any water safe to drink, and how to keep a household clean and healthy for one night, three days or a week without running water.

The rule it puts above the rest: if you are not sure water is safe, treat it before drinking, brushing teeth, washing food or making baby formula. It calls boiling the simplest method that works against germs almost everywhere.

If the tap has stopped, it sends you to the first ten minutes, while pressure is still in the pipes: drinking containers first, then the bath for flushing and washing, and the water heater off. If the water is running but may be unsafe, it sends you to “Is the water safe?” and a treatment card. The same Silva family appears here, two adults and two children in a fourth-floor flat in Lisbon.

It explains why a tap stops: a power cut and a building pump, a burst main, floods, drought, or an earthquake. On 28 April 2025, water kept flowing in many places, but some upper floors went dry when the pumps stopped. The lessons: fill everything in the first hour; keep drinking water separate from water for flushing and washing; knowing how to treat water is worth more than storing a lot. For the European Union’s 72 hours it uses about 12 litres per person, and shows how to stretch a household to a week, including boiling, chlorine, filters, cloudy water, water from outside, and rain.
