Fire, Gas and a Safe Home is the home-safety guide: what to do in a fire, a gas leak or a carbon monoxide alarm, and how to keep candles, heaters, stoves, batteries and generators from turning an outage into a disaster.

The rule it puts above the rest: get out, stay out, call 112. It says never to go back inside a burning building for belongings or pets, because smoke kills in minutes.

It explains why fires and carbon monoxide poisonings rise in a blackout, a cold snap or a storm: candles, gas heaters, camping stoves, generators and batteries come indoors, while mains alarms without a backup battery fall silent, lifts stop, and fire crews take longer. The lessons: a working smoke alarm on every floor; get out first and fight only a small fire, such as a pan or a bin; in an outage, check every flame and every battery.

If there is fire or a smell of gas, it says to stop reading, get everyone out, close the doors and call 112 from outside. The now pages are picture steps: escaping through smoke, trapped in a room, clothes on fire, a pan on fire, using an extinguisher, a smell of gas, a carbon monoxide alarm, and sparks or wet electrics. The prepare section starts with smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, then an escape plan, a fire blanket and an extinguisher, and the rules for candles, heaters, gas and lithium batteries. The minimum kit it names is a smoke alarm on every floor, a carbon monoxide alarm wherever something burns fuel, a fire blanket in the kitchen, a small extinguisher, torches instead of candles, and an escape plan. The households are the Silva family and their neighbours Joana and Sr. Alberto in a building in Lisbon, and Carlos and Inês in a house in the Alentejo.
