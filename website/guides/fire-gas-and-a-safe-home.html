Fire, Gas and a Safe Home is for the fires and the carbon monoxide poisonings that follow candles, heaters, camping stoves and batteries brought indoors in a blackout. Now is smoke, gas or an alarm already in the house, drawn like the card on a plane: everyone out, doors closed, 112 called from outside, and nobody back in for belongings or pets. A small pan fire is a different problem from a room filling with smoke. Prepare is how that night is less likely, alarms that work when the mains do not, a blanket and an extinguisher you can find in the dark, and an escape plan the household has walked. Reference is the printed set: safety cards, a check of the home, and a log of the equipment that has to work while people sleep. A Lisbon building and a house in the Alentejo are both here, because a flat and a house do not leave the same way. Most deaths in home fires are from smoke, often at night. The book is the minutes an alarm buys.
