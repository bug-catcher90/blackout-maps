Fire, Gas and a Safe Home is the book you want before a candle, a heater or a camping stove turns a blackout into a fire. If there is already smoke or a smell of gas, it tells you to stop reading, get everyone out, close the doors and call 112 from outside. It will not bend on that. Get out, stay out, and do not go back in for belongings or pets, because smoke kills in minutes and the people equipped to search are the firefighters.

The now pages are picture steps, like the safety card on a plane: escaping through smoke, trapped in a room, clothes on fire, a pan on fire, a small extinguisher used only on a small fire, a gas smell, a carbon monoxide alarm, and sparks or wet electrics. The prepare pages explain why these emergencies rise when the power is out. Candles, gas heaters, camping stoves, generators and charging batteries come indoors, while mains alarms without a backup battery go silent and fire crews take longer to arrive. You fit smoke alarms on every floor and a carbon monoxide alarm wherever fuel burns, then a fire blanket, an extinguisher, torches instead of candles, and an escape plan the household has practised. The examples follow a Lisbon building, the Silva family with their neighbours Joana and Sr. Alberto, and a house in the Alentejo, so both flats and houses are covered.
