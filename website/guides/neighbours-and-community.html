The first people to help are almost always the people next door. They are there long before a firefighter or a nurse can arrive.

Neighbours and Community starts with the first hour: make your own home safe, knock on the doors of people who live alone, check the stairs, share what you know, and hold a 15-minute meeting. It shows how to share water and power fairly, keep one place for news, help people leave, and calm things down when tempers rise.

Getting ready takes one evening. You map who lives where, with their consent, agree a few roles and a buddy for anyone vulnerable, and write down how the building will talk to itself and to local services. The examples follow a 20-flat building in Lisbon and a village in the Alentejo.

Inside

  • The first hour: who to knock on, the stairs, and a 15-minute meeting
  • Sharing water, food and power safely, and one place for news
  • Helping people leave, and what to do when tempers rise
  • A contact sheet, simple roles and a buddy for anyone who lives alone
  • Privacy, trust, and working with local services. Neighbours do not replace 112
  • Four situations: a three-day blackout, a heatwave, a wildfire near a village, and a flood clean-up
