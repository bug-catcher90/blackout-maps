The Go-Bag and Leaving Home starts with the decision, not the bag by the door. Staying home is usually the safer choice, and the book is clear about when that changes: when the authorities tell you to leave, when the building itself is the danger, and when waiting any longer puts you on a bad road. It uses the Pedrógão Grande fire of June 2017, when many of the people who died were caught in cars while trying to escape, to show why leaving late can be worse than staying. If you do have to move, you get a ten-minute routine and an hour-long one, by car or on foot, then how to find the way when the phone map is dead, where to go, and how to come home again.

The second half is the bag you hope not to need. It sets a weight you can actually carry, then works through documents, cash, medicines, and what changes for children, older people, people with disabilities and pets. The Silva family and their dog, Bolacha, are packed in full, with a car kit, routes that do not need a network, and a grab-and-go drill you time. Families are often split when trouble starts, at work, at school or on the road, so the meeting plan is on paper and does not depend on a phone. You finish with a bag that is ready and a clear sense of when to stay.
