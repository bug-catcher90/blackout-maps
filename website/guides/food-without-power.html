Food Without Power

Food Without Power is the food guide: what to eat first when the fridge goes warm, how to build a pantry that needs no cooking, and how to cook safely without electricity, for one night, three days or two weeks.

The rule it puts above the rest: when in doubt about food that has been warm, throw it out. Never taste food to check whether it is safe.

The fridge is the clock. In the first hour the book has you keep the fridge and freezer shut, note the time, switch off the oven and the hob, and plan today’s meals from the fridge rather than the cupboard. It follows Ana, Rui, Inês (8), Tomás (12) and a 12 kg dog in a fourth-floor flat in Lisbon. The order it teaches: fridge first, then freezer, then the cupboard.

It describes what fails in a long power cut. The fridge warms within hours, the freezer follows in one or two days, and electric hobs, ovens and microwaves stop at once. On 28 April 2025, card payments failed and bottled water, batteries and ready-to-eat food were the first things to go. Part A also covers meals with no cooking, cooking without power, babies, diets and medicines that need cold, and one shopping trip when the shops are closing and cards are down. Part B is the pantry: how much the household needs, food you can eat cold and already like, rotation so nothing is wasted, cooking with fuel, retained heat or a battery, and five household plans. It plans three days without cooking, a week with simple cooking, and two weeks for those who want more.
