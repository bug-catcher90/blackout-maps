Warm, Cool and Sheltered is for a cold house or a hot one when the power that usually fixes it is gone. In southern Europe, the book argues, heatwaves and cold homes kill more people than most storms, usually at home and often before anyone notices. Heatwaves in Portugal and Spain have caused hundreds or thousands of extra deaths, mostly among older people living alone, and poorly heated Portuguese homes have long meant a hard winter. Babies, older people and people who are ill are the ones to watch, because they often do not feel the danger themselves. You check on them twice a day, and you move them if the home cannot be made safe.

If it is cold now, you choose one room, close it off, and warm the people rather than the whole house, with layers, bedding, hot drinks and shared body heat. If it is hot, you shade the windows, drink, cool the skin with water, and open the place to night air. The prepare section explains how a home gains and loses heat, how to set up that warm room and that cool room, and which clothes, heaters and battery fans are worth having. It also covers a quick repair after a storm, sheltering from smoke, and the moment when somewhere else is safer than staying. The temperatures it uses come from the World Health Organization: at least 18 °C in cold weather, warmer for the most vulnerable, and indoor heat above about 32 °C by day or 26 °C at night treated as a risk. The same households run through it, the Silva family, Joana and Sr. Alberto in Lisbon, and Carlos and Inês in the Alentejo.
