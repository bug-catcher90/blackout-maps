Warm, Cool and Sheltered

Warm, Cool and Sheltered is the shelter guide: how to keep people safe in a cold home or a heatwave without power, make one room liveable, repair storm damage, shelter from smoke, and know when to go somewhere else.

The rule it puts above the rest: heat and cold are most dangerous for babies, older people and people who are ill, and they often do not notice it themselves. Check on them twice a day, and move them if the home cannot be made safe.

The book says that in southern Europe more people die from heatwaves and cold homes than from almost any other natural hazard, most of them at home. It describes heatwaves in Portugal and Spain causing hundreds or thousands of extra deaths, mostly among older people living alone, and Portugal’s high rate of extra winter deaths. The protection it teaches needs little power: layers, a closed room, hot drinks and shared body heat in the cold; shade, water on the skin, drinking and night air in the heat.

If it is cold, it starts by choosing one room, closing it off, and warming the people rather than the house. If it is hot, it starts with shade, drinking, cooling the skin, and checking on anyone older or unwell. It cites the World Health Organization figure of at least 18 °C in cold seasons, warmer for babies, older people and people who are ill, and indoor heat above about 32 °C by day or 26 °C at night as risky for vulnerable people. The prepare section is the warm room, the cool room, clothing and bedding, heaters and fans on a battery, quick repairs after a storm, and when to shelter somewhere else. The same households run through it: the Silva family, Joana and Sr. Alberto in Lisbon, and Carlos and Inês in the Alentejo.
