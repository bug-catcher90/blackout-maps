Staying Informed and in Touch is the communication guide: how to get reliable news, reach your family and call for help when phones, internet and power fail, and how to tell facts from rumours in a crisis.

The rule it puts above the rest: have a battery radio and know your local stations. When everything else fails, public radio is the channel the authorities use, and it needs no network, no account and almost no power.

On 28 April 2025, home routers went dark at once. Mobile networks carried on for a while on backup batteries, then became slow and patchy. The book says battery radios and the public broadcasters that stayed on air became the main source of reliable information, and that rumours spread in the gaps. The lessons: expect the network to fail; agree meeting points and check-in times before you need them; check information before you share it.

If the network is down now, it starts with the first hour: radio on, one phone saved, one short text to the family. The prepare section explains what fails first, which radio and walkie-talkies to buy, how to set up a phone, and a family plan that works with no network at all. The minimum kit it names is a battery or wind-up FM radio, a charged power bank, a paper list of phone numbers and radio frequencies, and a family plan on one sheet.
