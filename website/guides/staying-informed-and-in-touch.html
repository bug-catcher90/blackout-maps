Staying Informed and in Touch is for the hours when you cannot reach your children, your partner or the news. It follows what failed on 28 April 2025. Home internet went dark at once, mobile networks held for a while on backup batteries, and then calls and data became slow as everyone tried to get through at once. Public radio stayed on, and it became the channel people could trust. If the network is down now, the book’s first hour is small on purpose. Switch on a battery radio, save one phone, and send one short text to the family.

From there it shows how to keep getting official news, how to reach people when the network is overloaded, how to call for help, and how to check a rumour before you pass it on. The prepare section is the agreement you make before you need it. You learn what fails first, which radio and which local stations to rely on, how walkie-talkies fit a building or a street, and how to set a phone up so the battery lasts. A family plan with meeting points and check-in times means you can find each other with no network at all. The minimum it asks for is a battery or wind-up FM radio, a charged power bank, a paper list of numbers and frequencies, and that plan on one sheet.
