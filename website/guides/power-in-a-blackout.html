Power in a Blackout is for the evening the lights go out, and for the weekend you spend getting ready for the next one. If the power is already off, you can open it in the dark and follow one job per page, starting with the first ten minutes: who is stuck or hurt, light without candles, the cooker switched off, the fridge closed, and one phone saved for news. From there it stays with the problems that actually arrive, including cooking safely, the fridge and freezer, lifts and water in the building, people who depend on powered medical equipment, and what to do when the power comes back.

If you are preparing, it teaches the four words that make a plan possible, volts, amps, watts and watt-hours, then shows you how to measure a real day at home and replace the book’s example with your own. The example is the Silva family, two adults and two children in a flat, and every calculation in the book uses their numbers so you can check the working. You then choose a level, one night, three days or a week, with the European Union’s 72 hours in the middle, and match it to a power bank, a power station, a fuel generator or solar. The Iberian blackout of 28 April 2025 runs through the opening: the first hours were about people, and a charged power bank beat a large station that had never been tested. Tables, plug notes and forms to print sit at the back.
