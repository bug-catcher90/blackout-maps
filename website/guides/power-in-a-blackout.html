Power in a Blackout is the energy guide: what to do when the lights go out, and how to prepare light, phones, cold food and backup power for one night, three days or a week.

The book has three colour-coded parts. Now is for the outage itself, one job to a page, starting at “The first ten minutes”. Prepare is for the days before: volts, amps, watts and watt-hours, how to measure what a home uses, and a plan written on paper. Reference is tables, plugs and voltages, and forms to print. It follows the Silva family, two adults and two children in a flat, and says their numbers are examples to replace with yours.

It uses the blackout of 28 April 2025, when almost all of mainland Portugal and Spain lost power just after midday. Traffic lights, trains and metros stopped. People were stuck in lifts. Card machines and cash points stopped. Mobile networks became patchy, and phones went flat. The lessons it draws: the first hours are about people, not equipment; a charged power bank beat a large power station that had never been tested; after the first day the question is how much energy you have, how much you use, and when you can refill.

Part A covers light without fire, cooking safely, keeping one phone alive, the fridge and freezer, lifts and water in the building, people who depend on powered medical equipment, and what to do when the power comes back. Part B goes through power banks, power stations, fuel generators, recharging without the grid, solar, and five household plans. The European Union recommendation that households cope for at least 72 hours sits in the middle of the three levels the book plans for: one night, three days, and a week.
