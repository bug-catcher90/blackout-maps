First Aid When Help Is Slow

47-page PDF

€9.40

First Aid When Help Is Slow is for the minutes before an ambulance can reach you, and for the hours of care that may follow. On an ordinary day help is close. In a blackout, a storm, a fire or a flood, phones fail, roads block and every crew is busy, while people still have heart attacks, fall on dark stairs and burn themselves on camping stoves. The book is honest that it is not a course. Reading about CPR or a tourniquet is not the same as practising it, so it asks you to train with an instructor, then keep this guide to prepare and to remember. In an emergency you still call 112 and follow the operator, who can coach you while you wait.

The red pages are one emergency each, in the order that matters: not breathing, severe bleeding, choking, unconscious but breathing, chest pain and stroke, burns, allergy and asthma, heat and cold, and whether to call, go, or wait. A few actions taken in the first minutes, starting CPR, stopping heavy bleeding, laying an unconscious person on their side, cooling a burn, and giving 112 the right information, are what the book expects you to be able to do before any special kit. The blue pages are the household side of the same problem: where to take a course in Portugal, what belongs in the kit, which medicines to keep, and how to look after wounds, broken bones, fever and dehydration through a long wait. The examples follow the Silva family, Joana and her neighbour Sr. Alberto, and Carlos and Inês in the Alentejo.
