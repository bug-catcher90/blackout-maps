First Aid When Help Is Slow is the medical guide: what to do in the first minutes of a medical emergency, how to care for injuries and illness while you wait, and the kit, medicines and skills a household should have.

The book says it is not a course. Reading about CPR or a tourniquet is not the same as practising it. It says to use the guide to prepare and remind, to take a practical first aid course, and to call 112 and follow the operator.

It is written for the hours when an ambulance is not a few minutes away: a blackout, a storm, a fire or a flood, when phones may fail and roads may be blocked. The actions it names, before any special equipment, are starting CPR, stopping heavy bleeding, putting an unconscious person on their side, cooling a burn, and getting the right information to 112.

The red pages are emergency cards in order of urgency: not breathing, severe bleeding, choking, unconscious but breathing, chest pain and stroke, burns, allergy and asthma, too hot or too cold, and whether to call, go, or wait. The prepare section covers a first aid course, the kit, medicines, wounds, broken bones, burns, fever, dehydration and the long wait. It names INEM, the Portuguese Red Cross, municipalities, fire brigades and employers as places in Portugal that run first aid and CPR courses. The households are the Silva family in Lisbon, Joana and her neighbour Sr. Alberto, and Carlos and Inês in the Alentejo.
